Interface size scales every screen — Live TV, VOD, Catch-up and this one. It never changes the video itself, so fullscreen playback is unaffected.

Streams are tried with each player in turn until one works. Pick a default if one player is reliably better on your TV — it's tried first, and the others still act as fallbacks. The RED button cycles players during playback.

Video output

Older LG sets draw video on a separate hardware layer that can't be rounded or clipped, which shows up as a black Live TV preview box with working sound. Compatibility mode draws the preview square and unclipped to fix that. Automatic already picks it for webOS 5 and below — change this only if the preview is black, or if it's fine and you'd rather have the rounded corners back.

Multiview (the BLUE button on Live TV) plays several channels at once. Every TV has a limited number of video decoders — ask for more than it has and the extra tiles stay black, sometimes taking the working ones with them. Automatic uses 4 on webOS 6 and newer and 2 below that. Lower it if tiles go black; raise it if your TV copes.

How subtitles look, and where to get them when your provider doesn't supply any. Position and timing are adjusted during playback instead — press the subtitles button on the player, since both only make sense while you can see the result.

Appearance
OpenSubtitles

Most providers embed subtitles in the video rather than supplying separate files, and this TV's decoder often can't read those. Searching OpenSubtitles sidesteps the provider entirely. Needs a free API key from opensubtitles.com/consumers. Adding your account login as well raises the daily download limit.
